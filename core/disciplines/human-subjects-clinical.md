# Human-subjects clinical research

What a researcher from outside the field needs before a draft or a review touches
work done with human participants in a clinical setting: how the field weighs
evidence, which words mean what, what its referees ask first, and what its ethics
require. Generic by construction: it describes the field, never a project.

Source notation. Every item ends with its source and a verification note in the
`LITERATURE_MAP.md` form, `verified: {how, on, by}`. `read-full-text` means the
page or document was fetched and the cited passage read on the date given;
`abstract-only` and `metadata-only` mean exactly that; `unverified` means no
source could be read and the item stands on the card's own authority only. Statute
and guideline locators are written as "Article N", "Paragraph N", "Principle N",
"Item N", "Point N", "Recommendation N", "Section N"; Chinese terms are given
bilingually where the working word in Taiwan is Chinese.

## Evidence conventions

- **Pre-registered endpoints.** The primary and secondary outcomes are written into
  the protocol and a public registry before the first participant is enrolled; the
  report names the registry, the record and the registration date, and any outcome
  or analysis that was not prespecified is reported as a change made after the
  trial commenced, never silently. Sources: Declaration of Helsinki (October 2024
  revision), Paragraph 35 (registration "in a publicly accessible database before
  recruitment of the first participant"), https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; ICMJE clinical
  trial registration policy (registration "at or before the time of first patient
  consent for enrollment" as a condition of publication),
  https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/publishing-and-editorial-issues/clinical-trial-registration.html
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; CONSORT 2025
  expanded checklist, Item 2 (trial registration), Item 14 (prespecified primary
  and secondary outcomes), Item 10 (changes after the trial commenced, including
  any outcomes or analyses that were not prespecified),
  https://www.consort-spirit.org/_files/ugd/b5740e_a6856e5e2cf94a1db5a8005853404160.pdf
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **A screening score is not a diagnosis.** A screening test sorts apparently
  healthy people into "probably has" and "probably does not have"; "the screening
  test is not always intended to be diagnostic", and "those screened 'positive'
  often need another confirmatory procedure or test". A cut-off score therefore
  "screens positive"; only the confirmatory work-up diagnoses. Source: Aggarwal,
  Ranganathan, Pramesh, "Research studies on screening tests", Perspectives in
  Clinical Research (2022), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9345258
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}. The classic WHO
  monograph by Wilson and Jungner (1968) states the same principle — verified:
  {how: unverified, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable} (the WHO repository could not be
  reached; the statement is not quoted here).
- **A screening or correlational result is "associated with", never "detects".**
  Cause-and-effect wording ("effect", "reduces", "detects", "causes") is reserved
  for designs that permit causal assessment — randomised trials and controlled
  experiments; cohort, cross-sectional, case-control and case-series designs
  report association, defined as a relationship "in which one does not necessarily
  cause the other"; "when two variables are measured simultaneously, association
  rather than causation generally is all that can be assessed". Sources: AMA
  Manual of Style, as set out in the AMA Style Insider post "The Use of
  Cause-and-Effect Language in the JAMA Network Journals",
  https://amastyleinsider.com/2017/09/19/use-cause-effect-language-jama-network-journals
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; STROBE checklist
  Item 20 ("a cautious overall interpretation of results considering objectives,
  limitations, multiplicity of analyses, results from similar studies"),
  https://www.strobe-statement.org/download/strobe-checklist-cohort-case-control-and-cross-sectional-studies-combined
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **Effect sizes are reported with uncertainty, per group, with the sample size
  justified in advance and harms assessed systematically.** For each outcome: the
  result for each group and the estimated effect size with its precision (a
  confidence interval), the distinction between prespecified and post-hoc
  analyses, the sample-size determination, and how harms were defined and
  assessed; observational reports give unadjusted and confounder-adjusted
  estimates with their precision and discuss bias and imprecision as limitations.
  Sources: CONSORT 2025 expanded checklist, abstract item ("a result for each
  group and the estimated effect size and its precision"), Item 16a (sample
  size), Item 15 (harms), Item 21a ("effect measure ... with confidence
  intervals"; "distinction between prespecified and post-hoc analyses"),
  https://www.consort-spirit.org/_files/ugd/b5740e_a6856e5e2cf94a1db5a8005853404160.pdf
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; STROBE checklist
  Item 16 ("unadjusted estimates and, if applicable, confounder-adjusted estimates
  and their precision"), Item 19 (limitations: "sources of potential bias or
  imprecision"), Item 21 (generalisability),
  https://www.strobe-statement.org/download/strobe-checklist-cohort-case-control-and-cross-sectional-studies-combined
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; STROBE statement,
  von Elm and colleagues, PLoS Medicine (2007),
  https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.0040296
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **What distinguishes routine care from a research procedure is purpose, not
  novelty.** "Practice" refers to interventions "designed solely to enhance the
  well-being of an individual patient or client and that have a reasonable
  expectation of success"; "research" designates "an activity designed to test an
  hypothesis, permit conclusions to be drawn, and thereby to develop or contribute
  to generalizable knowledge", usually described in a formal protocol; a novel
  procedure "does not, in and of itself, constitute research", and where practice
  and research occur together the activity is the kind that "ought to undergo
  review for the protection of human subjects". In Taiwanese law, human research
  (人體研究) is any obtaining, investigating, analysing or using of human
  specimens or of a person's biological, behavioural, physiological,
  psychological, genetic or medical information for research. Sources: Belmont
  Report (1979), Part A "Boundaries Between Practice and Research",
  https://archive.epa.gov/hsrb/web/pdf/belmont_report.pdf — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; 人體研究法 (Human Subjects Research
  Act), Article 4, https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020176&flno=4
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; Declaration of
  Helsinki, Paragraph 14 (physicians involve their patients in research only to
  the extent justified by its potential value) — verified: {how: read-full-text,
  on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **Negative and inconclusive results are published or otherwise made public,
  as found.** Source: Declaration of Helsinki, Paragraph 36 — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **Qualitative and mixed designs carry their own evidence conventions** — see
  "Methodology switch".

## Terminology

Each entry: term (中文 / English) — meaning in this field — the near-miss it must
not be confused with — source.

- **失智症 / dementia; 失智照護 / dementia care** — a syndrome of acquired cognitive
  decline that is a disease, not normal ageing ("失智症是一種疾病現象而不是正常的老化");
  the working word in Taiwanese health-authority usage. Near-misses: **老人癡呆／
  癡呆症**, the older label that advocacy groups urged replacing with 失智症 from the
  late nineteen-nineties and that is retired because of stigma — never use it;
  **正常老化 / normal ageing**, the lay misreading that delays diagnosis and feeds
  stigma; **認知障礙症 / major neurocognitive disorder**, the diagnostic-manual label
  (adopted in some other Chinese-speaking jurisdictions) — in Taiwan the working
  word stays 失智症. Sources: 衛生福利部 press release for World Alzheimer's Day
  (treating dementia as normal ageing "延誤診斷治療的先機"; ignorance lets patients
  "背負污名或備受歧視"), https://www.mohw.gov.tw/cp-3558-37591-1.html — verified:
  {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; 《失智症診療手冊》 (Dementia
  Clinical Handbook, third edition; supervising unit 衛生福利部醫事司; executing unit
  台灣失智症協會) — naming history ("倡議將「癡呆症」改為「失智症」"), stigma, and the
  diagnostic-manual renaming to 認知障礙症,
  https://www.mohw.gov.tw/dl-27189-8993c3ad-0f47-45e0-a602-6a4362faae9a.html
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; 花蓮縣衛生局 dementia
  page ("不是正常的老化"), https://www.hlshb.gov.tw/News_Content.aspx?n=2112&s=14676
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **可識別資料 / identifiable data; 編碼資料 / coded (pseudonymised) data; 去連結資料 /
  de-linked (anonymised) data** — identifiable: information that directly or
  indirectly identifies a natural person; coded (pseudonymised): processed so it
  "can no longer be attributed to a specific data subject without the use of
  additional information" kept separately — and still personal data, because it
  "could be attributed to a natural person by the use of additional information";
  去連結 (de-linked): research material coded or otherwise processed so that it can
  "永久不能以任何方式連結、比對" with identifying data. Near-miss: a coded dataset is
  not anonymised; only 去連結 lifts the Article 19 duties of destruction and
  re-consent. Sources: 個人資料保護法 (Personal Data Protection Act), Article 2
  (identify "directly or indirectly"), Article 6 (medical records, healthcare,
  genetics and physical examination as specially protected categories),
  https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=I0050021 — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; 人體研究法, Article 4 (去連結),
  https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020176&flno=4 — verified:
  {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; GDPR Article 4 ('personal
  data', 'pseudonymisation'), https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/, and Recital 26
  (pseudonymised data remain identifiable; anonymous information is outside the
  principles), https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-26/ — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **同意 / consent vs 本人同意（贊同） / assent** — consent is the legally effective
  agreement of a person with capacity (the Act's default participant is a
  "有意思能力之成年人"); assent is the affirmative agreement of someone who cannot
  legally consent, and "mere failure to object should not, absent affirmative
  agreement, be construed as assent". In Taiwan a 限制行為能力人 or a 受輔助宣告之人
  gives their own consent and the 法定代理人 or 輔助人 consents as well (both,
  "應得其本人及法定代理人或輔助人之同意"). Near-miss: silence, compliance or a
  representative's signature is not the person's assent. Sources: 人體研究法,
  Article 12, https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020176&flno=12
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; US Common Rule,
  Subpart D, definition of "assent" (Cornell LII copy),
  https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/46.402 — verified: {how: read-full-text,
  on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; ICH E6(R3), Principle 2 ("assent should be collected
  from that minor, as appropriate") and Annex 1, "Informed Consent of Trial
  Participants" (age-appropriate assent information; the participant signs the
  consent or assent form "if capable"),
  https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6%28R3%29_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106.pdf
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **代理同意 / proxy (surrogate) consent; 法定代理人 / legal representative; 監護人 /
  guardian; 輔助人 / assistant; 關係人 / statutory relatives; legally authorised
  (acceptable) representative** — for a 無行為能力人 or 受監護宣告之人 the legal
  representative or guardian consents; for an adult admitted under the Article 12
  exception (research of clear benefit to a specific population, or no
  replaceable participants) consent comes from relatives in the statutory order
  spouse, adult children, parents, siblings, grandparents — one relative's written
  consent suffices and disagreement is resolved by that order and by closeness of
  kinship and co-residence. Near-misses: the family member who happens to be
  present is not automatically the lawful consenter; proxy consent does not cancel
  the participant's own dissent. Sources: 人體研究法, Article 12 — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; Declaration of Helsinki, Paragraph 28
  (consent "from the legally authorized representative") and Paragraph 29 ("the
  potential participant's dissent should be respected") — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; ICH E6(R3), Principle 2 ("legally
  acceptable representatives, acting in the participants' best interest") —
  verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **篩檢 / screening vs 診斷 / diagnosis** — screening sorts asymptomatic people by
  probability; diagnosis is the confirmatory judgement that follows a positive
  screen. Near-miss: a screening instrument's cut-off "screens positive"; it does
  not "diagnose", and a study of a screening score reports association with the
  reference diagnosis, not detection of the disease. Source: Aggarwal and
  colleagues (2022), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9345258 — verified:
  {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **常規醫療（臨床照護） / routine care vs 研究程序 / research procedure** — routine
  care is done solely for the individual's benefit; a research procedure is done
  to answer the protocol's question, however ordinary it looks. Near-miss: novelty
  does not make a procedure research, and familiarity does not make a research
  procedure care. Source: Belmont Report, Part A — verified: {how: read-full-text,
  on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **相關（關聯） / association vs 效果 / effect** — association: a relationship
  between two variables "in which one does not necessarily cause the other";
  "effect" is limited to designs that permit assessment of causal findings (the
  statistic "effect size" is the one exception). Source: AMA Style Insider post
  on cause-and-effect language — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03,
  by: fable}.
- **附帶發現 / incidental findings vs 次要發現 / secondary findings** — incidental
  findings lie outside the aim of the test or procedure; secondary findings are
  deliberately sought beyond the primary aim. Near-miss: disclosing an incidental
  finding "does not transform a research relationship into a clinical one".
  Source: Presidential Commission for the Study of Bioethical Issues, "Anticipate
  and Communicate" (December 2013), Research Recommendations,
  https://www.genome.gov/Pages/PolicyEthics/HealthIssues/Anticipate_Communicate.pdf
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **意思能力 / decision-making capacity** — the Act admits "有意思能力之成年人" as the
  default participant; a working test of capacity is the ability to understand the
  relevant information, retain it, use or weigh it in deciding, and communicate the
  decision, and a person "is not to be regarded as unable to understand" if an
  explanation "appropriate to his circumstances (using simple language, visual aids
  or any other means)" lets them understand. Near-miss: a dementia diagnosis is
  not a capacity finding; capacity is decision-specific and assessed for the
  decision at hand (this gloss is the card's; the sources give the test and the
  default). Sources: 人體研究法, Article 12 — verified: {how: read-full-text, on:
  2026-11-03, by: fable}; UK Mental Capacity Act (2005), Section 3,
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/section/3 — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **弱勢族群 / vulnerable individuals and groups** — people whose circumstances
  impair free consent or raise the cost of harm; research with them "is only
  justified if it is responsive to their health needs and priorities", and
  Taiwanese policy guidance admits minors and the vulnerable only where the research
  clearly benefits their collective or individual interests and the legal
  representative or closest relation has been informed and consents in writing.
  Near-miss: vulnerability calls for safeguards, not blanket exclusion. Sources:
  Declaration of Helsinki, Paragraph 20 — verified: {how: read-full-text, on:
  2026-11-03, by: fable}; 人體研究倫理政策指引 (衛生署 policy guidance, 2007),
  Point 6, https://www.mohw.gov.tw/dl-16084-2ccb7514-de3f-4e29-a747-27dce4b0c435.html
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **人體研究倫理審查委員會（審查會） / institutional review board (IRB), research
  ethics committee (REC); 研究主持人 / principal investigator; 受試者同意書 / informed
  consent form** — the committee that must approve the protocol before research
  begins and every amendment before it is implemented; the person who submits the
  protocol and answers for it; the document that records what Article 14 requires
  the participant to be told. Near-miss: a 簡易審查 (expedited review) or a
  得免審查 exemption is a category the competent authority announces, not a status
  the investigator declares. Sources: 人體研究法, Article 5,
  https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020176&flno=5, and
  Article 14, https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020176&flno=14
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; 衛生福利部醫事司
  "人體研究相關業務" page listing the announced ranges (得簡易程序審查, 得免取得研究對象同意,
  得免倫理審查委員會審查), https://dep.mohw.gov.tw/DOMA/fp-2782-9538-106.html —

## What reviewers in the field ask

- **Is the principal investigator eligible, and has the team the ethics training
  the rules require?** For trials under the Medical Care Act the investigator must
  hold a practising licence with the clinical experience the regulation sets, have
  the training hours in human research and in medical ethics within the window it
  sets, and have no disciplinary bar; for other human research each IRB sets its
  own hours (verify locally); grant rules add an academic-ethics course for
  first-time investigators and require the ethics approval document with the
  application; GCP asks that everyone involved be "qualified by education,
  training and experience to perform their respective task(s)". Sources:
  人體試驗管理辦法, Article 4, https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020162&flno=4
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; 國家科學及技術委員會
  補助專題研究計畫作業要點, Point 26 (academic-ethics education for first-time
  investigators) and Point 11 (ethics-committee approval attached to an
  application involving human research), https://law.nstc.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?id=FL026713
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; ICH E6(R3),
  Principle 5 and Annex 1, "Qualifications and Training" — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; the per-IRB hours for non-trial
  research — verified: {how: unverified, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable} (institutional
  SOPs were found by search and differ; none is cited here).
- **What does the research add beyond routine care, and what burden does it add?**
  The consent process exists to let participants "evaluate the benefits, risks and
  burden of participating"; the protocol must separate the procedures that are
  care from the procedures that are research and state the foreseeable risks and
  the remedy if harm occurs. Sources: ICH E6(R3), Principle 2 — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; Belmont Report, Part A — verified:
  {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; 人體研究法, Article 14
  (foreseeable risks and remedies among the items that must be explained) —
  verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **Who is in the room during a procedure?** Qualitative reporting asks "was anyone
  else present besides the participants and researchers?" and who conducted the
  session; GCP requires an impartial witness "present ... during the entire
  informed consent discussion" when the participant or representative cannot read.
  Sources: COREQ, Item 15 (presence of non-participants), Item 1
  (interviewer/facilitator), Item 14 (setting), https://academic.oup.com/intqhc/article/19/6/349/1791966

### Register of an IRB protocol

- An IRB protocol is a terse answer in the template's register, not an essay: it
  "should describe the nature of your project, the potential human subjects risks,
  and the human subjects protection plan", section by section in the order the
  form asks. Source: NBER IRB protocol format,
  https://www.nber.org/protocol-format-nber-irb-submissions — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- Copy the template's standard sentences and keep the template's headings; the
  consent document "should follow the template", with optional elements "used if
  appropriate or deleted if not". Source: Clarkson University IRB protocol
  guidelines, https://intranet.clarkson.edu/wp-content/uploads/IRB_Protocol_Guidelines_10252018.pdf
  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- State only this study and the protection of its participants. A grant proposal
  may look ahead to later aims; a protocol may not: "do not attach and reference a
  grant proposal; include appropriate content within the IRB proposal", and "it is
  no longer necessary to submit the grant, for comparison, with the IRB
  application". Sources: Clarkson guidelines and NBER format, as above —
  verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; the "may look ahead
  / may not" contrast is the card's gloss on those two sources, not a quotation.
- Write every risk, procedure and data step consistently across protocol, consent
  form and recruitment material; the IRB reads them side by side. Source: ICH
  E6(R3), Annex 1, "Communication with IRB/IEC" (documented approval of the
  protocol, consent materials, recruitment procedures "and any other trial-related
  information to be provided to participants") — verified: {how: read-full-text,
  on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.

## Ethics and consent norms

- **Approval before any participant contact.** The investigator "實施研究前，應擬定
  計畫，經倫理審查委員會 ... 審查通過，始得為之"; the protocol "must be submitted for
  consideration, comment, guidance, and approval to the concerned research ethics
  committee" before the study begins; recruitment procedures and advertisements are
  part of what is approved, because advertising "is the start of the informed
  consent and subject selection process". Sources: 人體研究法, Article 5 —
  verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; Declaration of
  Helsinki, Paragraph 23 — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by:

  — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **Consent forms at a junior-high reading level.** Taiwanese guidance for writing
  a 受試（訪）者同意書: narrative, colloquial wording that a person who has completed
  basic education — the third year of junior high — can understand ("以國三 ...
  程度 ... 能夠理解為原則"), avoiding Chinese and English technical terms, and not
  merely copying the protocol; the statute requires disclosure "以研究對象 ...
  可理解之方式"; consent information "shall be in language understandable to the
  subject or the legally authorized representative" and begins "with a concise and
  focused presentation of the key information"; participants are "adequately

  understandable". Sources: 臺北市政府 "受試(訪)者同意書書寫注意事項及填寫說明",
  https://www-ws.gov.taipei/001/Upload/446/relfile/22076/9096693/9f8ecd39-4e2b-4ce5-a0af-fd468fa4e3f8.odt

## Methodology switch

### Quantitative (trials and observational designs)

- **Evidence conventions:** registration before enrolment; prespecified outcomes;
  effect measure with confidence interval per group; sample size justified;
  harms assessed; non-prespecified analyses labelled post hoc; observational
  findings reported as association with cautious interpretation. Sources:
  CONSORT 2025 statement, Hopewell and colleagues, BMJ (2025),
  https://www.bmj.com/content/389/bmj-2024-081123 — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; CONSORT 2025 expanded checklist,
  Items 2, 10, 14, 15, 16a, 21a — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03,
  by: fable}; STROBE checklist, Items 16, 19, 20, 21 — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; AMA Style Insider on cause-and-effect
  language — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **Reporting:** the CONSORT checklist for a randomised trial, the STROBE
  checklist for cohort, case-control and cross-sectional studies; the registry
  record cited in the abstract. Sources as above.
- **Review questions:** was the primary outcome fixed before unblinding; does the
  interval exclude no effect; were harms sought actively; is the language causal
  only where the design permits it. Sources as above.

### Qualitative (interviews, focus groups, observation)

- **Evidence conventions:** credibility rests on transparency about who collected
  the data and under what relationship, where, with whom present, whether
  recorded, how saturation was judged, and whether participants checked the
  findings; the researcher's "bias, assumptions, reasons and interests in the
  research topic" are reported, not hidden. Source: COREQ (Tong, Sainsbury,
  Craig, International Journal for Quality in Health Care, 2007), Items 1, 6, 8,
  14, 15, 19, 22, 28, https://academic.oup.com/intqhc/article/19/6/349/1791966 —
  verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **Reporting:** COREQ for interview and focus-group studies; SRQR, a
  twenty-one-item standard that "aims to improve the transparency of all aspects
  of qualitative research", for qualitative reports generally. Source: SRQR
  (O'Brien and colleagues, Academic Medicine, 2014),
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24979285/ — verified: {how: abstract-only, on:
  2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **Review questions and ethics:** who was in the room; who heard the recording
  and under what agreement; whether the interviewer held authority over the
  participant; whether quotations can identify a speaker. Sources: COREQ Items 1,
  6, 15, 19 — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; UMBC
  guidance on transcriptionists — verified: {how: read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03,
  by: fable}; Declaration of Helsinki, Paragraph 27 — verified: {how:
  read-full-text, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}.

### Mixed methods

- **Evidence conventions:** the mixed design is justified and described
  (purpose, priority, sequence); each strand meets its own standard — the
  quantitative strand per CONSORT or STROBE, the qualitative strand per COREQ or
  SRQR; integration of the strands is attempted and shown, since "judgements
  about integration could rarely be made due to the absence of an attempt at
  integration" in the reviewed literature. Source: GRAMMS (O'Cathain, Murphy,
  Nicholl, Journal of Health Services Research and Policy, 2008),
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18416914/ — verified: {how: abstract-only, on:
  2026-11-03, by: fable}.
- **Reporting:** GRAMMS as the minimum (justification, design, each method's
  quality, where integration occurred, limitations of one method associated with
  the other, insights from mixing) — the six items beyond the abstract —
  verified: {how: unverified, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}; the APA JARS–Mixed
  table as an alternative — verified: {how: unverified, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable}
  (the page could not be read).
- **Review questions:** why mix; which strand leads; where exactly the strands
  meet; what the qualitative strand adds that the numbers cannot carry and the
  reverse; whether the two strands' consent, data-handling and retention duties
  were each met (the quantitative strand's coded dataset and the qualitative
  strand's recordings are distinct materials under Articles 14 and 19). Sources:
  GRAMMS abstract — verified: {how: abstract-only, on: 2026-11-03, by: fable};
  人體研究法, Articles 14 and 19 — verified: {how: read-full-text, on:
  2026-11-03, by: fable}.
