# Collaboration proposal — A daily stretching routine and lower-back discomfort among warehouse pickers

Submitted to: Greywick Depot
Submitted by: Marlow University — M. Okafor, Assistant Professor, Department of Occupational Health, Marlow University
Date: 2026-11-03

## 1. Summary

This proposal asks Greywick Depot to host the study described below. The study will test whether a supervised four-minute stretching routine at the start of each shift reduces self-reported lower-back discomfort among warehouse pickers compared with usual practice. It will involve 48 participants over 12 weeks. Participation is voluntary and may be withdrawn at any time without consequence for the participant's employment.

## 2. Background

Pickers at Greywick Depot already answer a weekly wellbeing survey that records a discomfort score. This proposal asks whether a supervised four-minute stretching routine at the start of each shift lowers that score compared with usual practice. The routine takes four minutes of shift time; the primary outcome is the score on the survey the site already runs; and the two groups compared are the site's own shift teams. The comparison will be pre-registered before the first enrolment, and either answer — a lower score in the stretching group, or no difference — will be reported. ## 3. Aim

The study will test whether a supervised four-minute stretching routine at the start of each shift reduces self-reported lower-back discomfort among warehouse pickers compared with usual practice.

## 4. Design

Participants: 48 (24 per arm), recruited at one site (Greywick Depot).
Duration: 12 weeks from first enrolment to last follow-up.
Primary outcome: the discomfort score on the site's existing weekly wellbeing survey, recorded weekly.
Allocation: two arms (stretching routine; usual practice), allocated by shift team (cluster allocation).
