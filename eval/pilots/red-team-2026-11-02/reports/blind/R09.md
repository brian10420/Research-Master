### A1 (load-bearing) — The headline significance claim is false on the ledger's own numbers

Claim attacked (RESEARCH_STATE.md:37-41):
> "The difference, +0.013, meets the pre-registered margin (0.010) and the Welch t-test is
> significant at α = 0.05 (p = 0.04, df ≈ 3.1)."
Repeated at HYPOTHESES.md:20 ("outcome: confirmed (+0.013, p = 0.04; RESEARCH_STATE C1)").

The hole: the p-value is wrong; the decision rule flips.

Recomputation (ledger rows EXPERIMENT_LEDGER.md:26-28 and :30-32, test nDCG@10):
- in-batch 0.503 / 0.509 / 0.491 → mean 0.5010, sample sd 0.0092
- Kestrel 0.512 / 0.498 / 0.531 → mean 0.5137, sample sd 0.0166
- difference +0.0127; Welch se = sqrt(0.0092²/3 + 0.0166²/3) = 0.0109
- t = 0.0127 / 0.0109 = 1.159; Welch df = 3.12 (the file's "df ≈ 3.1" is correct)
- two-sided p = 0.33 (one-sided 0.16); 95 % CI on the difference [−0.022, +0.047]
- exact permutation test on the mean difference: 8 of 20 relabellings are as extreme → p = 0.40
- paired by seed (both arms share initialisation, RESEARCH_STATE.md:70): differences
  +0.009 / −0.011 / +0.040, paired t = 0.85, df 2, p = 0.48

No mis-specification I tried reproduces 0.04: pooled-variance t (p = 0.31), se = sd/n
instead of sd/√n (p = 0.135; one-sided 0.067), se = sqrt((s1²+s2²)/6) (p = 0.20), one-sample
t against the in-batch mean (p = 0.32), margin-subtracted t (p = 0.82). The df matches
Welch exactly, so the test was set up correctly and the p was mis-read, mis-typed or
computed by a different script than the source of record.

Supporting evidence that the effect is one seed: leave-one-seed-out means
- drop seed 0: Kestrel 0.5145 vs in-batch 0.5000 (+0.0145)
- drop seed 1: 0.5215 vs 0.4970 (+0.0245)
- drop seed 2: 0.5050 vs 0.5060 (−0.0010)
Kestrel seed 1 (0.498, EXPERIMENT_LEDGER.md:31) is below two of the three in-batch seeds.
The +0.013 mean is carried by RUN_008's +0.040.

Consequence under the project's own rule: HYPOTHESES.md:17 "confirmed iff mean difference
>= 0.010 and Welch p < 0.05 (two-sided); refuted otherwise" → with p = 0.33, H_001 is
refuted. C1 cannot go to submission as a confirmed claim; RESEARCH_STATE.md:20-21
("P1 comparison complete; claims below proposed for submission lock") rests on it.

Cheapest test: run `scripts/report_p1.py` at code_rev `e13f7a9` (RESEARCH_STATE.md:10,
:24, :61) and diff its printed p against RESEARCH_STATE.md:41. Cost: minutes, no GPU. If the
script prints 0.04 the script has a bug and every number it emits is suspect; if it prints
0.33 the state file was hand-edited and the source-of-record rule (PROTOCOL.md:49-50) was
broken. Either way the pre-registered outcome is "refuted".

### A3 (load-bearing) — The n = 3 design cannot confirm the margin it pre-registers

Claim attacked (PROTOCOL.md:46-48; HYPOTHESES.md:17, :33; PROTOCOL.md:38-44):
> "Welch two-sided t-test on per-seed test metric (n = 3 per arm), α = 0.05, superiority
> margin 0.010."

The hole: with the seed variances the ledger actually shows (sd 0.009 and 0.017), the
smallest difference that clears two-sided p < 0.05 at n = 3 is 0.034 — 3.4× the margin.
The decision rule "difference ≥ 0.010 AND p < 0.05" can therefore only ever fire for an
effect more than three times the size the hypothesis asks about. H_001 was not
confirmable at the moment it was pre-registered; H_003 (same rule, same n, PROTOCOL.md:42-44)
inherits the same defect. This is independent of A1: fixing the p-value does not fix the
design, and re-running RUN_010 under it will not produce a confirmable C3.

Recomputation (observed variances held fixed; "smallest significant diff" = difference at
which the Welch test just reaches p = 0.05, i.e. ~50 % power; the 80 %-power column adds
the usual t_0.80 shift):

| seeds per arm | se | smallest diff with p < 0.05 | diff for ~80 % power |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 0.0109 | 0.034 | 0.044 |
| 5 | 0.0085 | 0.021 | 0.028 |
| 8 | 0.0067 | 0.015 | 0.021 |
| 10 | 0.0060 | 0.013 | 0.018 |
| 16 | 0.0047 | 0.010 | 0.014 |
| 20 | 0.0042 | 0.009 | 0.012 |

Cheapest test: more seeds, or an honest downgrade. (a) Downgrade at zero cost: report C1 as
"+0.013, 95 % CI [−0.022, +0.047], not significant at n = 3". (b) To make the *observed*
+0.0127 significant, ~10 seeds per arm (7 more per arm = 14 runs); at the ~8–9 GPU-h per
learned run implied by "about 60 GPU-hours" for H_001's eight rows (HYPOTHESES.md:18),
≈ 120 GPU-h. To detect a true 0.010 with 80 % power, ~20 per arm (≈ 300 GPU-h). Neither
fits before 2026-11-19 together with RUN_010 unless the budget changes; the operator
should choose (a) or move the deadline, not launch RUN_010 at n = 3 and hope.

### A5 (minor) — "Same budget" hides an ~8× asymmetry in negatives per step

step and wall-clock per run from the RUN_002–RUN_008 logs in the limitations; minutes.
Whether this is the cause of the gain is exactly H_003 (A2).

### A8 (minor) — The mining step is under-specified for replication and for C3

re-sampled — which 16 of the top-100, whether judged-relevant passages (grade ≥ 1) are
filtered out of the cache (false-negative control), and whether hard negatives are shared
across the batch are all unstated. These determine what H_003's "uniformly sampled
negatives (same cache size)" is actually controlling for. Cheapest test: read
`cfg/kestrel.yaml` and `cfg/kestrel_randneg.yaml` and write the three facts into
PROTOCOL.md §2 before RUN_010 launches; minutes.
