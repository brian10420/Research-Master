## 1. Verdict

HOLES: 3 load-bearing, 3 minor

Worst: the three arms are not statistically independent across seeds — Cutout equals
standard aug + 0.53 ± 0.02 on every seed (r = 0.9997) and Lumen-Mix equals standard aug
+ 1.13 ± 0.06 (r = 0.984), a coupling the ledger does not record and that either signals a
shared pipeline artefact or, if real, makes the pre-registered unpaired test the wrong
test and puts the whole paired interval for the Cutout comparison below the 0.8 margin.

## 2. Ranked attacks

### A1 — Per-seed results are near-perfectly coupled across arms; "Anomalies: none" is false (load-bearing)

Claim attacked:
- `EXPERIMENT_LEDGER.md:50`: "Anomalies: none recorded in RUN_001–RUN_010 logs beyond the
  RUN_008 preemption."
- `PROTOCOL.md:37`: "Welch two-sided t-test on per-seed test top-1 (n = 3 per arm)" — the
  unpaired test that C1 (`RESEARCH_STATE.md:40-41`) and C2 (`RESEARCH_STATE.md:42-44`) rest on.

The hole: the per-seed test top-1 values (`EXPERIMENT_LEDGER.md:24-33`) are
standard aug 91.12 / 90.87 / 91.35, Cutout 91.64 / 91.38 / 91.90, Lumen-Mix 92.31 / 91.95 / 92.48
(seeds 11 / 22 / 33). Recomputed:

| pair | per-seed differences (11 / 22 / 33) | sd of differences | sd expected if arms independent | Pearson r across arms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cutout − std | 0.52 / 0.51 / 0.55 | 0.021 | 0.354 | 0.9997 |
| Lumen-Mix − std | 1.19 / 1.08 / 1.13 | 0.055 | 0.362 | 0.984 |
| Lumen-Mix − Cutout | 0.67 / 0.57 / 0.58 | 0.055 | — | 0.979 |

The within-arm spread is 0.24–0.27 per arm, yet the between-arm difference per seed varies
by 0.02–0.06. The seed ordering (22 < 11 < 33) is identical in all three arms (chance 1/36
under independence; alone not damning, but the magnitudes are). For ResNet-20-class
training the seed-to-seed variance is dominated by the whole trajectory, not by the
initial weights, so a cross-arm correlation of 0.98–0.9997 under three different
augmentation pipelines is not what independent runs produce.

Why it is load-bearing, whichever way it resolves:
1. If it is an artefact (shared cached evaluation, the same checkpoint scored under three
   names, a seed that also fixes the test-time pipeline, a scoring script bug), every
   number in C1–C3 is suspect.
2. If the seed effect is real, the design is paired by seed and the pre-registered unpaired
   Welch test (`PROTOCOL.md:37`) is mis-specified. A seed-paired analysis (df = 2,
   t_crit = 4.303) gives:
   - Lumen-Mix − std: mean 1.133, 95 % CI [0.996, 1.270], p = 0.0008 — C1 gets stronger.
   - Lumen-Mix − Cutout: mean 0.607, 95 % CI [0.470, 0.744], p = 0.003 — the whole interval
     lies below the 0.8 margin. Under the pre-registered rule (`HYPOTHESES.md:25`) the
     outcome stays "inconclusive", but the data actually exclude the H_002 statement
     ("at least 0.8 points over Cutout", `HYPOTHESES.md:24`). C2's "no claim ... stops
     there" (`RESEARCH_STATE.md:44-45`) is then too generous to the method, and a reviewer
     who pairs by seed will say so.

Cheapest test (zero GPU, under an hour): (a) read `train.py` / the config loader for what
the seed fixes (init only, or also the sampler order and the eval pipeline); (b) pull the
per-epoch validation curves of RUN_001–RUN_010 and check whether the 22 < 11 < 33
ordering holds at every epoch of every arm, and whether the test scores come from distinct
checkpoint files (hashes). Confirming test if (a)–(b) are clean: two further seeds
(44, 55) on standard aug and Lumen-Mix only, 4 runs ≈ 12 GPU-h; if the paired-difference
sd stays ≈ 0.05 the coupling is real and the paper reports the paired analysis as the
sensitivity analysis; if it jumps to ≈ 0.3 the first nine rows need an explanation.

### A3 — "Confirmed: at least 0.8 points" is not what the interval supports (load-bearing, cheapest to fix)

Claim attacked:
- `HYPOTHESES.md:16`: "statement: with ResNet-20 at the P1 budget, Lumen-Mix raises test
  top-1 by at least 0.8 points over flip + crop"; `HYPOTHESES.md:20`: "outcome: confirmed
  (+1.13, p = 0.006 ...)".
- `RESEARCH_STATE.md:40-41`: "The difference, +1.13 points, meets the pre-registered margin
  (0.8) and the Welch t-test gives p = 0.006 (df ≈ 3.9)."

The hole: the decision rule (`HYPOTHESES.md:17`, `PROTOCOL.md:30-31`) is "mean difference
>= 0.8 and Welch p < 0.05", where the p-value tests difference ≠ 0, not difference ≥ 0.8.
Recomputed Welch: diff 1.133, se 0.209, t = 5.43, df = 3.94, two-sided p = 0.0058,
t_crit = 2.792, 95 % CI [0.550, 1.717]. The interval contains every value from 0.55 up;
the one-sided test of H0: δ ≤ 0.8 gives t = 1.60, p = 0.093. The data establish δ > 0
and estimate δ ≈ 1.13; they do not establish δ ≥ 0.8. The rule as pre-registered is
satisfied (the point estimate is above 0.8), so this is not a protocol breach — it is a
wording overclaim attached to the headline hypothesis, and `RESEARCH_STATE.md:28` sends
the paper-writer to "draft the results section from this file and the ledger only", so
the phrase "at least 0.8 … confirmed" propagates unless corrected here. The same rule is
reused verbatim for H_004 (`HYPOTHESES.md:41`), so the defect is forward-looking too.

Cheapest test: none needed — the recomputation above is the test (cost zero). The fix is
text: C1 reports "+1.13 (95 % CI 0.55–1.72), p = 0.006; the pre-registered point-estimate
margin of 0.8 is met", and the H_001 outcome says "confirmed under the point-estimate
rule; the interval does not exclude gains below 0.8". If the authors want "at least 0.8"
as a statement, the pre-registration for H_004 should test H0: δ ≤ 0.8 directly.

### A4 — Asymmetric tuning: the method's one knob was tuned on val, Cutout's one knob was not (minor)

Claim attacked:
- `PROTOCOL.md:44-45`: "lr, wd and batch size are the Marigold-32 reference recipe for
  ResNet-20 and are the same for all arms; no per-arm tuning."
- `PROTOCOL.md:46-48`: "Lumen-Mix has one extra knob (mixing strength). It was fixed at 0.4
  from a preliminary single-seed sweep on the validation split".
- `PROTOCOL.md:12`: "Cutout: standard aug + one 16x16 cutout patch (p = 1.0)."
- C4 (`RESEARCH_STATE.md:50-51`): "the baselines are standard augmentation and Cutout".

The hole: "no per-arm tuning" is true of lr/wd/batch only. The method had a validated
sweep of its sole hyperparameter on this dataset; Cutout's sole hyperparameter (patch
size) was transplanted at 16×16 with no sweep — 16 is the CIFAR-10 value, and the
original Cutout work used a smaller patch for a harder label space, which Marigold-32
(20 classes, `PROTOCOL.md:4`) may be. The sweep's own table (which seed, which strengths,
which val numbers) is declared outside the ledger (`PROTOCOL.md:47-48`), so a reviewer
cannot see how far 0.4 is from the next-best value. Standard aug has no knob, so C1 is
untouched; C2's +0.61 and C4's "baselines" wording are what this reaches. Minor because
C2 claims nothing.

Cheapest test: disclose the sweep table (zero cost); or a 3-point single-seed val sweep of
Cutout patch size {8, 12, 16} ≈ 9 GPU-h, reported as a limitation check, not as a claim.

### A5 — Two primary tests at α = 0.05 with no family-wise control; C2's p = 0.049 is a reading hazard (minor)

Claim attacked:
- `RESEARCH_STATE.md:42-44`: "the observed difference is +0.61 points, below the
  pre-registered margin of 0.8 (Welch p = 0.049)".
- `PROTOCOL.md:29-33`: two pre-registered comparisons, each at α = 0.05
  (`PROTOCOL.md:37`).

The hole: recomputed C2 Welch: diff 0.607, se 0.217, t = 2.80, df = 3.99, p = 0.0489,
95 % CI [0.005, 1.209]. At a Bonferroni α/2 = 0.025 it is not significant, and the
interval's lower end is 0.005. Printing "p = 0.049" beside "+0.61" with no claim invites
the reader to supply the claim ("marginally significant improvement over Cutout").
Minor because C2 as written claims nothing; the hazard is in what a reader does with the
number.

Cheapest test: zero cost — report C2 as the interval [0.01, 1.21] and either drop the
p-value or state that two primary tests were run uncorrected.

### A6 — The 0.8 margin is unjustified and the design is powered only at that margin (minor)

Claim attacked:
- `PROTOCOL.md:37-38`: "α = 0.05, superiority margin 0.8 points".
- `RESEARCH_STATE.md:65-66`: "the C2 comparison is not decidable at this n, which is why
  C2 makes no claim".

The hole: no file says where 0.8 came from (practical significance, prior work, or
power). With per-arm sd ≈ 0.25 (recomputed 0.24–0.27) and n = 3, the approximate power
of the pre-registered test is 0.84 at δ = 0.8, 0.56 at δ = 0.6, 0.98 at δ = 1.13. So
"inconclusive" for C2 is the design's predictable output for any true gain under ≈ 0.8,
not a finding about Cutout. The authors say this in effect (`RESEARCH_STATE.md:65-66`);
the paper should say it with the power number. Minor: pre-registered, honestly flagged.

Cheapest test: zero cost — one sentence giving the power at the margin and the rationale
for 0.8.
